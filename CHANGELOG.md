## [Unreleased]

### Fixed

- **Strategy: "merge" reappears after split** ([#59](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/59)) — clicking **split** on a parent class (Alternatives, Fixed Income, Developed Intl) distributed its weight into sub-class targets but then permanently hid the **merge** link, leaving no way to undo the split. The merge button is now suppressed only when a child class is *held* in the portfolio (you can't hide real positions); a split that only set targets with no current holdings is always reversible.

### Added

- **Expanded asset-class hierarchy — Phase 2a** ([#36](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/36)) — three new allocation dimensions built on the generalized hierarchy below, all opt-in via the portfolio-driven visibility rule (sub-classes stay hidden until held or individually targeted). **Fixed income** is now a parent class that subdivides into `US_TREASURY`, `US_CORP_BOND`, `MUNI_BOND`, `TIPS`, and `INTL_BOND` — the highest-value addition, since each sleeve places by tax treatment (munis in taxable; taxable-coupon bonds shelter in tax-advantaged) instead of one blended bucket. **Developed international** subdivides into `DEVELOPED_EUROPE` and `DEVELOPED_PACIFIC` (≈ MSCI EAFE 62/38). **Real estate** (`REAL_ESTATE`, REITs) is a new standalone top-level sleeve defaulting to tax-advantaged placement. Common ETFs/mutual funds for every new sleeve are mapped (e.g. GOVT/VGIT → Treasury, LQD/VCIT → corporate, MUB/VTEB → muni, SCHP/VTIP → TIPS, BNDX → intl bond, VGK/VPL → Europe/Pacific, VNQ/SCHH → real estate); `MUNY` now maps to `MUNI_BOND`. A user holding a broad bond or developed-international fund against a parent-level target sees no change — holdings roll up and consolidate to the parent, and the sub-classes surface only when explicitly used. Default split weights are anchored on index composition data (Bloomberg US Aggregate, MSCI EAFE) and are fully user-editable.
- **Hierarchy: opt-in subdivision on load** ([#36](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/36)) — parent-level targets for classes legitimately held at the parent level (fixed income, developed international, alternatives) are now preserved on load instead of being auto-split into sub-classes. Only synthetic parents never held at the parent level (US total market → cap sub-classes) still auto-split. This keeps a simple "20% bonds" allocation from silently fanning out into five bond sleeves the user never chose.
- **Generalized asset class hierarchy** ([#36](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/36)) — the engine now supports a declarative parent/child relationship between asset classes. The `CLASS_HIERARCHY` map in `src/engine/hierarchy.ts` is the single source of truth; rollup, resolve, and schema migration all consume it generically. `ALTERNATIVES` is now a parent class with two children: `CRYPTO` (IBIT, FBTC, GBTC, ETHA) and `PRECIOUS_METALS` (GLD, IAU, GLDM, SLV, SGOL). Users who target `ALTERNATIVES` at the parent level continue to get parent-level trade recommendations; users who hold specific sub-class funds see those classes surface automatically via the portfolio-driven visibility rule. A **parent-class consolidation pass** in the engine ensures sub-class holdings count toward the parent target when only the parent is targeted, preventing spurious "sell PRECIOUS_METALS / buy ALTERNATIVES" trades for users who simply hold GLD against an `ALTERNATIVES` target. Greenfield buy recommendations are promoted to the parent level when no sub-class positions exist in the account, so the engine never suggests "buy CRYPTO + buy PRECIOUS_METALS" when the user simply wants ALTERNATIVES exposure. The hierarchy now surfaces consistently across the UI: the Classify dropdown nests sub-classes under their parent; the Strategy **Target allocation** card renders any parent group (not just US Total Market) with indented sub-class rows and the same linked/composite handle — including a **split** control to break a parent target into its sub-classes and a **merge** control to fold them back; the Strategy **Placement rules** card indents sub-classes under their parent and defaults them to the parent's placement preference; and the Results **Household allocation** table indents sub-classes beneath their parent. Sub-class rows stay hidden until they actually carry weight (held or individually targeted), keeping simple portfolios uncluttered.

- **Tax-adjusted allocation** ([#7](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/7)) — opt-in mode that values each account at its after-tax worth before computing drift and target sizes, so a 60/40 target refers to after-tax wealth rather than nominal market value. Roth, HSA, and 529 count at face value; Traditional / 401k balances are haircut by your expected effective rate at retirement (typically lower than your working-age marginal); taxable positions are haircut by the LTCG on their unrealized gain. Cost basis is per-position (auto-imported from Schwab and Fidelity CSVs, manually editable in the Import tab for taxable accounts only). When enabled, the Strategy panel exposes the rate inputs plus a two-row stacked-bar visualization comparing nominal and after-tax composition by asset class, the Results panel adds **After-tax current** and **After-tax projected** columns alongside nominal weights and shows after-tax household value in the header, and the engine drives trade sizing against the after-tax target. A new `MISSING_COST_BASIS` flag fires for any taxable position lacking basis (engine falls back to no haircut, conservatively understating tax drag).
- **Aggressiveness × diversification plot** ([#16](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/16)) — the Household allocation card on the Results tab now includes a 2D scatter plot showing the portfolio's aggressiveness (share in equities & alternatives) against its diversification (normalized inverse Herfindahl across asset classes). A blue arrow from the current point to the projected-after-trades point visualizes the impact of the rebalance, with a dashed green marker for the strategy's target.
- **Next-rebalance reminder** ([#19](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/19)) — set a reminder date for your next rebalance on the Results tab. Pick a preset interval (3 / 6 / 12 months) or any custom date; the Results tab shows an amber/red banner when the reminder is within seven days or overdue. "Mark rebalanced today" stamps the last-rebalanced date and, if an interval was chosen, auto-advances the next due date. "Add to calendar" opens a dropdown with one-click deep links to **Google Calendar, Outlook.com, Office 365, and Yahoo** that pre-fill the event, plus an **Apple Calendar / .ics** download for everything else — all client-side, no calendar API calls.
- **Blended funds are tradeable as a unit** ([#40](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/40)) — target-date funds, balanced funds, and any user-defined decomposed holding are now eligible to be sold whenever the household drift requires it (no longer treated as permanently frozen). The fund's relative composition stays fixed; only the total amount changes. The engine prefers selling non-blended positions first, falling back to a proportional sale of the blended fund only when non-blended capacity can't absorb the needed change. Where proportionality forces over-selling a sub-class, the engine routes a compensating buy via another ticker in the same account so the per-account net stays balanced. A new `BLENDED_FUND_OVERSELL` flag fires when no compensation path exists (e.g. an account whose menu carries no non-blended ticker for the over-sold class), surfacing the residual drift to the user. The position-level `locked` flag remains the escape hatch for "don't touch this specific blended-fund holding." A new informational `BLENDED_FUND_SOLD` flag — surfaced in the Results panel and the exported HTML report — discloses that the engine sold part of a blended fund this run, since there is no symmetric buy-back mechanism and repeated rebalances will trend the allocation away from any blended-fund position the engine has touched.
- **5/25 relative tolerance band** ([#2](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/2)) — `Strategy.toleranceRel` (default 25%) sets a second tolerance band as a fraction of each class's target. The engine now uses whichever band is tighter per class (Bogleheads / Swedroe rule): the absolute band dominates for large sleeves, the relative band dominates for small sleeves. Existing strategies without `toleranceRel` default to 25%.
- **Strategy panel** — new "Relative tolerance" input alongside absolute tolerance, with help-box explanation of the 5/25 rule.
- **"No preference" placement option** ([#21](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/21)) — an asset class can now be set to "No preference" in the placement rules, letting the engine place it wherever account capacity is largest without flagging the result as a spillover. Useful for tax-efficient, broadly-held classes that work in any account type.
- **Retirement drawdown / spending mode** ([#14](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/14)) — raise cash from the portfolio by selling. Total withdrawal = **Σ RMDs** (required minimum distributions, entered manually per account) **+ an additional discretionary amount**. The engine takes RMDs first, then drains withdrawal-eligible accounts in a per-account **drain-priority** order (taxable → traditional → Roth by default, editable), selling down toward the target allocation. Each account gains a three-way **withdrawal eligibility** control — **Restricted / Eligible / Required** (Required reveals an RMD input) — defaulting to Restricted for tax-advantaged accounts (early-withdrawal penalties) and Eligible for taxable. Planned deposits (free cash) and withdrawals run independently in the same pass, netting per account. The Accounts tab shows planned deposits and withdrawals side by side; the Results tab and exported report show a spending-plan table. A new `WITHDRAWAL_SHORTFALL` flag fires when the additional amount can't be fully raised.

### Changed

- **Strategy panel** — the Target allocation and Placement rules cards are now collapsible; click the section heading to show or hide the card.
- **Per-account deposit limits** ([#30](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/30)) — account deposit eligibility is now a three-way choice: **Open** (any share of household free cash), **Limited** (capped at a dollar amount), or **Closed** (none). Use "Limited" when a contribution limit is partly consumed — e.g. a Roth IRA with room left this year. Each account is clamped at its limit, and overflow spills to accounts with remaining room; anything that still can't be placed surfaces as `EXCESS_CASH`. An account's own new cash always deploys in full regardless of the limit. The Accounts table, Add/Edit forms, Results cash-deployment table, and exported HTML report all show the new state.
- **Demo sample portfolio rescaled to ~$100k** — the bundled synthetic fixture (loaded via `?demo` or the Import tab) now totals ~$100k of net worth instead of ~$1M, sized to feel familiar to a typical early- or mid-career saver. The five accounts, drift scenario, and every demonstrated feature (locked positions, account restrictions, concentrate rules, ticker override, blended fund, new cash, household free cash, deposit-closed account) are preserved at the smaller scale. `minTradeUsd` lowered from $250 to $100 to match the new portfolio size.
- **Free-cash routing now concentrates instead of spreading** ([#37](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/37)) — household free cash is deployed into the single largest-capacity eligible account first, spilling to additional accounts only when a deposit limit forces it. Previously it was split across all eligible accounts in proportion to their value, which touched more accounts and generated more trades without improving the allocation. This minimizes the number of accounts traded (in line with the tool's trade-minimization goals).

### Fixed

- **Trade list granularity matches pie chart** ([#36](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/36)) — when the strategy targets child asset classes individually (e.g. `CRYPTO: 1.5%, PRECIOUS_METALS: 1%`), the trade card now shows separate child-level buy recommendations instead of a single collapsed parent-level "Alternatives" trade. The greenfield-buy promotion that collapses all-sibling unresolved buys to the parent is now gated on whether the strategy is operating at parent granularity; child-level targets suppress promotion so the trade list stays in sync with the projected-weights pie chart.
- **Sub-class placement** ([#36](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/36)) — the placement engine iterated a hardcoded class list for ordering and silently skipped any asset class not enumerated there, so a directly-targeted hierarchy sub-class (e.g. `CRYPTO`, `MUNI_BOND`) could be left unplaced with no trades generated. Placement now orders every asset class, using the hardcoded list only to break ties.
- **Robustness to partial strategies** ([#36](https://github.com/rblumen/drift/issues/36)) — `rebalance` now normalizes its target map so a strategy object missing a class key treats it as 0% rather than producing `NaN` drift, which previously zeroed out every trade. Schema-parsed strategies were already complete; this hardens direct engine callers.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-05-29

### Added

- **Rebalancing engine** (`src/engine/`) — pure TypeScript, fully unit-tested household-level rebalancer covering target-weight drift detection, greedy asset-class placement across account tiers (taxable / tax-advantaged / education), new-cash deployment to underweight classes, and absolute tolerance / minimum-trade filtering.
- **Ingest layer** (`src/ingest/`) — CSV adapters for Empower, Schwab, and Fidelity brokerage exports normalised into a single `Position` schema; manual entry fallback; institution-aware field parsing.
- **Five-panel UI** — Import, Classify, Strategy, Results, and Overview tabs wired end-to-end to the engine.
- **Strategy panel** — editable target weights with linked US cap-class sub-allocation, per-asset-class placement rules, tolerance band and minimum-trade controls, and new-cash input.
- **Results panel** — household allocation before/after side-by-side pie charts, per-account trade cards, engine flag annotations (`TAXABLE_SALE`, `REVISIT_PLACEMENT`, `UNMAPPED_HOLDING`, `PROJECTED_OFF_TARGET`, `WASH_SALE_RISK`, etc.), and a downloadable HTML report.
- **Placement ordering** — placement tier preference derived dynamically from strategy constraints rather than a fixed hierarchy; greedy fill concentrates trades into the fewest accounts and drops marginal trades that fall within tolerance.
- **Persistence** — auto-save to localStorage on every change; JSON export/import for manual backup; full portfolio reset.
- **Demo mode** — `?demo` URL parameter loads a synthetic sample portfolio for exploration without real data.
- **Engine flag: `PROJECTED_OFF_TARGET`** — warns when post-rebalance weights are projected to remain outside tolerance, with a stress-test suite.
- **GitHub Actions CI** — Vitest runs on every push and pull request.
- **Vercel deployment** — preview and production deploy configuration.

### Architecture

- Engine is framework-free; UI and ingest depend on the engine, never the reverse.
- All computation is client-side — no backend, no network calls, no telemetry.
- Synthetic fixtures only; real financial data is git-ignored.
